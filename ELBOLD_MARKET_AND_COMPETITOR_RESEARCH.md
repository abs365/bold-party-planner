# ELBOLD Market & Competitor Research — Section 1

**Scope:** UK and international event-vendor marketplaces and service-business operating platforms.
**Core question this research serves:** Why would an event professional who already runs a private CRM/diary/spreadsheet/website move part of their business to ELBOLD and keep paying monthly — including in months with zero marketplace leads?
**Date prepared:** July 2026.
**Method:** Live web research (official pricing/product pages, Trustpilot/G2/Capterra reviews, industry commentary). Every claim below is sourced with a URL. Where a competitor does not publish pricing, or where sources conflict, this is stated explicitly rather than estimated as fact.

---

## How to read this report

Thirteen platforms are assessed: the nine named in scope (Hitched, Bark, Bridebook, Add to Event, HoneyBook, Dubsado, Studio Ninja, Eventbrite, Square Appointments) plus four additional genuinely comparable platforms (Poptop, Tagvenue, 17hats, Thumbtack) chosen because they represent business models ELBOLD directly competes with or must differentiate from: UK live-marketplace commission models (Poptop, Tagvenue), a pure pay-per-lead marketplace at global scale (Thumbtack), and a second CRM-only comparator (17hats) to triangulate the "operational lock-in" pattern against HoneyBook/Dubsado/Studio Ninja.

Each profile covers: core promise, target customer, pricing, marketplace/CRM/booking/contracts/payments/automation/profile/reviews/analytics capability, subscription justification, strengths, weaknesses, what ELBOLD must not copy, and the market gap left open. A comparison table and cross-cutting synthesis follow the profiles.

## 1. Hitched (owned by The Knot Worldwide / XO Group)

**Core promise:** The UK's largest wedding directory — get discovered by engaged couples searching for suppliers by category and location.

**Target customer:** UK wedding suppliers (photographers, venues, caterers, florists, etc.) seeking brand visibility and inbound enquiries from couples.

**Pricing model:** Hitched does not publish a public pricing page for suppliers — pricing is sales-negotiated and opaque. Trustpilot and industry reports place costs anywhere from roughly £175/month for standard professional memberships up to £250/month reported by one photographer for a premium listing, with some annual renewal quotes as high as £1,600–£5,000+/year including VAT ([Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/www.hitched.co.uk), [Chris Giles Photography review](https://www.chrisgilesphotography.com/advertising-wedding-photographer-advertising-with-hitched-review/)). **This figure could not be independently verified against an official rate card; treat as directional only.**

**Marketplace capability:** Yes — passive directory listing plus paid tiers for elevated placement and featured positioning; couples contact suppliers via "Request for Information" (RFI) forms.

**CRM capability:** Minimal — basic enquiry/contact management and "performance analytics" for supplier accounts, not a true CRM ([search summary](https://leadiq.com/c/hitched/5a1da8692300005c009c4658)).

**Booking capability:** None found — no native booking/calendar/availability system; enquiries are handled off-platform.

**Contracts and invoicing:** Not offered.

**Payment capability:** Not offered (couples do not pay suppliers through Hitched).

**Automation:** Limited to "automated lead routing" of RFIs to suppliers; no workflow automation for the supplier's business.

**Public profile or storefront:** Yes — full supplier profile pages are the core product.

**Reviews and verification:** Couple reviews are a feature of supplier profiles (standard for this category).

**Analytics:** Vendors get "performance analytics" and account success manager support at higher tiers, but no self-serve dashboard details were found publicly.

**Subscription justification without leads:** Weak. The subscription buys visibility, not operational tooling — if enquiry volume drops, the supplier is paying purely for a listing they could have kept for free or at a much lower tier, with no fallback business utility. This is a documented pain point in supplier reviews.

**Strengths:** Dominant UK brand recognition; large organic couple traffic; part of a global network (The Knot Worldwide) giving cross-market reach.

**Weaknesses:** Opaque, high-pressure sales-driven pricing; no operational tooling; supplier reviews cite aggressive renewal upselling and difficulty controlling spend; zero utility once the wedding season/lead flow dips.

**What ELBOLD must not copy:** Opaque, negotiated pricing with no public rate card; renewal/upsell pressure tactics reported by suppliers; a subscription with zero fallback utility when leads are low.

**Market gap left open:** Suppliers have no way to "keep the value" of a Hitched subscription when leads dry up — the money is pure sunk cost. Any platform that lets a supplier extract ongoing operational value (CRM, bookings, invoicing) from the same subscription closes this gap directly.

## 2. Bark.com

**Core promise:** Get matched with local customers looking for your service, across virtually every trade/category (not wedding/event-specific).

**Target customer:** Broad — services businesses generally (tradespeople, tutors, event suppliers, etc.) across UK, US, Ireland, Canada, South Africa.

**Pricing model:** No subscription. Pay-per-lead via a credit system: professionals buy credit packs and spend credits to unlock contact details/respond to a lead. UK credits cost roughly £1.20+VAT each; leads typically require 4–30 credits depending on job value/trade, translating to roughly £5–£45 per lead contacted ([Bark pricing](https://www.bark.com/en/us/sellers/pricing/), [Bark Help Centre](https://help.bark.com/hc/en-gb/articles/13346288068892-What-is-a-credit-and-how-much-does-it-cost)). Credits expire after 3 months. No commission on jobs won.

**Marketplace capability:** Yes — the entire product. Leads are shown with cost-to-contact displayed upfront.

**CRM capability:** None found beyond basic message/lead tracking within the platform.

**Booking capability:** None.

**Contracts and invoicing:** Not offered.

**Payment capability:** Not offered — Bark takes no cut of the job itself, professionals are paid directly by the customer.

**Automation:** None found.

**Public profile or storefront:** Basic profile pages exist to build trust with prospective customers.

**Reviews and verification:** Customer reviews of professionals are a core trust feature.

**Analytics:** None found publicly documented.

**Subscription justification without leads:** N/A by design — there is no subscription. This is Bark's central structural advantage and threat: professionals pay nothing when there's no opportunity worth pursuing.

**Strengths:** Zero fixed cost; transparent per-lead pricing shown before purchase; "hire guarantee" refund mechanism (first pack only); broad category coverage.

**Weaknesses:** Very poor reputation in review aggregation — 1.2/5 average across 223 reviews on PissedConsumer and broadly low scores on Trustpilot; widespread complaints of fake/unresponsive leads, credits expiring before use, and a one-time-only refund guarantee not disclosed upfront ([PissedConsumer](https://bark-com.pissedconsumer.com/complaints/RT-P.html), [SmartCustomer](https://www.smartcustomer.com/reviews/bark.com)).

**What ELBOLD must not copy:** Charging for the mere opportunity to respond regardless of lead quality; a refund guarantee that is effectively single-use and poorly disclosed; credit expiry windows that convert unused spend into pure profit.

**Market gap left open:** No accountability loop connecting what a supplier is charged to what they actually win — a marketplace that only charges (or charges materially less) on genuine, verified booking outcomes would directly address Bark's biggest reputational liability.

## 3. Bridebook (Bridebook Business)

**Core promise:** The UK's leading wedding planning app for couples, with a supplier-facing "Bridebook Business" arm offering visibility, enquiry management, and light CRM tools.

**Target customer:** UK wedding suppliers and venues, segmented by category (photographers, caterers, planners, venues, etc.).

**Pricing model:** Published, tiered subscription pricing (verified from official pages, photographer example): **SupplierPro Basic** £20/month (or £60/quarter, £240/year, all +VAT); **SupplierPro Classic** £99/month base rate, discounted to ~£93/month quarterly and ~£86.13/month annually (£1,033.50+VAT/year) ([Bridebook pricing](https://partners.bridebook.com/uk/pricing/suppliers/photographers)). A free "Limited Profile" tier also exists for unsubscribed businesses. Other categories (venues, caterers, planners) have separate rate cards on the same domain.

**Marketplace capability:** Yes — profile discovery plus paid "Couples Targeting" and priority search placement at the Classic tier.

**CRM capability:** Light — enquiry inbox, auto-response templates (2 on Basic, 4 on Classic), enquiry templates, and Instagram integration at Classic tier. Not a full client/project CRM.

**Booking capability:** None found — no calendar/availability/scheduling system.

**Contracts and invoicing:** Not offered.

**Payment capability:** Not offered.

**Automation:** Auto-response templates only; not workflow automation.

**Public profile or storefront:** Yes, core product — "Enhanced profile listing" at both paid tiers.

**Reviews and verification:** Yes — couples can leave reviews; suppliers can respond to reviews at the Classic tier.

**Analytics:** "Couple Activity" tracking is offered but depth is unclear from public materials.

**Subscription justification without leads:** Weak-to-moderate. Some suppliers report strong enquiry volume ("incredibly strong" leads per reviews), but subscription value is still tied almost entirely to lead flow and search placement — nothing to use if enquiries pause. Bridebook Trustpilot reviews are mixed on the supplier side despite a high overall (consumer-skewed) rating; documented complaints include fake enquiries, unremovable defamatory reviews, and spam ([Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/bridebook.com)).

**Strengths:** Official, published pricing (rare in this category — a trust signal); genuine planning-app usage by couples (not just a directory); tiered pricing allows suppliers to start cheap.

**Weaknesses:** CRM is a thin layer bolted onto a directory, not a real operating system; review/dispute handling reportedly weak; £99/month Classic tier is a meaningful fixed cost for a marketplace-only value proposition.

**What ELBOLD must not copy:** Treating CRM features as marketing add-ons to a listing product rather than a genuine operational core; weak recourse for suppliers disputing fake enquiries or unfair reviews.

**Market gap left open:** Bridebook proves UK wedding suppliers will pay £20–£99+/month for a directory with *some* CRM veneer — but nobody has offered them a *real* CRM (contracts, invoicing, payments, availability) bundled with marketplace access at a comparable price point.

## 4. Add to Event

**Core promise:** The UK's leading marketplace connecting event suppliers (parties, weddings, corporate) directly with people actively planning events in their area.

**Target customer:** UK event suppliers across a broad category range — entertainment, catering, equipment hire, etc.

**Pricing model:** Free to sign up and browse local requests; pay-as-you-go credits to contact a specific lead, with credit cost varying by job value. NCASS-affiliated suppliers get 75 free credits (~£82 value) as a new-member incentive; a 75-credit bundle carries a 20% NCASS discount ([Add to Event – how it works](https://www.addtoevent.co.uk/how-it-works/suppliers), [NCASS partnership](https://www.ncass.org.uk/add-to-event/)). No commission on bookings won. Supplier reviews report an effective cost around £5 per lead ([review synthesis](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/www.addtoevent.co.uk)).

**Marketplace capability:** Yes — core product; filterable by category and travel radius, with hundreds of daily requests distributed to relevant suppliers.

**CRM capability:** None beyond basic company profile fields (name, address, services, description).

**Booking capability:** None — booking/negotiation happens off-platform after initial contact.

**Contracts and invoicing:** Not offered.

**Payment capability:** Not offered.

**Automation:** Notification-based lead alerts matched to supplier preferences; not broader workflow automation.

**Public profile or storefront:** Basic supplier profile exists, browsable at addtoevent.co.uk.

**Reviews and verification:** NCASS "verified" badge available; standard review functionality.

**Analytics:** None found publicly documented.

**Subscription justification without leads:** N/A — no subscription; the same structural advantage as Bark. Suppliers pay nothing when idle.

**Strengths:** Genuinely zero fixed cost; large, active UK request volume claimed ("hundreds of daily requests"); no commission on won business; trade-association (NCASS) credibility layer.

**Weaknesses:** Reviews show a sharp split — requesters (couples/event organisers) rate it very highly, while suppliers report the model favours the cheapest bidder and that per-lead costs (~£5 each) add up quickly against inconsistent conversion, with some describing it as difficult to win business unless lowest-priced ([Trustpilot supplier feedback](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/www.addtoevent.co.uk)).

**What ELBOLD must not copy:** A structure that implicitly rewards race-to-the-bottom pricing among suppliers competing for the same lead; charging for contact regardless of whether the requester is a serious buyer.

**Market gap left open:** Same as Bark/Thumbtack pattern — no platform in this set differentiates suppliers on business quality/reliability rather than price when leads are distributed, and none gives the supplier anything to fall back on operationally between leads.

## 5. Poptop

**Core promise:** The UK's first true "live marketplace" for event suppliers — instant pricing and availability, direct online booking, no back-and-forth quoting.

**Target customer:** UK party/event suppliers (entertainers, caterers, mobile bars, equipment hire) serving weddings, private parties, corporate events.

**Pricing model:** No membership/listing fee to appear on the platform — suppliers set services and pricing for free. Poptop takes a **12% commission on the full booking value** once a booking is confirmed, with funds released roughly 14 days before the event; suppliers choose a deposit rate between 12–100% ([Poptop blog](https://www.poptop.uk.com/blog/poptop-first-event-marketplace-uk/)).

**Marketplace capability:** Yes — core product; over 10,000 services listed, live instant pricing/availability search by date, occasion, guest count, postcode.

**CRM capability:** Minimal — messaging and booking management within the platform, not a general CRM.

**Booking capability:** Yes — this is Poptop's key differentiator versus Bark/Add to Event/Hitched: instant, transactional booking rather than a quote-request model.

**Contracts and invoicing:** Booking terms are handled through the platform's transaction flow; no evidence of standalone contract-generation tooling for suppliers' wider business.

**Payment capability:** Yes — deposits and balances are processed through Poptop as part of the booking flow (this is required for the commission model to function).

**Automation:** None found beyond the booking/payment flow itself.

**Public profile or storefront:** Yes — supplier profile pages with live pricing.

**Reviews and verification:** Strong — 4.8★ aggregate on Trustpilot from 56k+ verified post-event reviews, and Poptop states it never edits or removes reviews as part of its "Client Protection" promise ([Poptop reviews](https://www.poptop.uk.com/reviews/), [Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/poptop.uk.com)).

**Analytics:** Not documented publicly beyond booking history.

**Subscription justification without leads:** N/A — no subscription, commission-only. However, one documented supplier complaint states that even after deleting all services, Poptop continued charging to "promote" an empty profile and refused refunds — a red flag around auto-renewal/cancellation friction.

**Strengths:** True transactional marketplace (payment + booking, not just lead-gen); no fixed cost; strong review volume and reputation; built-in escrow-like deposit handling reduces no-show/non-payment risk for suppliers.

**Weaknesses:** 12% commission on every booking is a meaningful, uncapped ongoing tax on revenue with no ceiling; reported difficulty cancelling/avoiding charges after deleting services; couples cannot contact a supplier directly until a deposit is paid, which can create a "locked in before you've really engaged" dynamic that some clients dislike.

**What ELBOLD must not copy:** Uncapped percentage commission with no volume discount structure evident; billing/cancellation friction after a supplier has removed their services.

**Market gap left open:** Poptop proves suppliers will accept commission-based payment processing if it removes chasing/no-show risk — but a flat, uncapped 12% forever is expensive at scale. A platform offering payment/booking infrastructure with commission that tapers (or is replaced by a flat subscription) as volume grows would out-compete this on total cost for established vendors.

## 6. Tagvenue

**Core promise:** A venue-hire marketplace — list your venue free, get discovered for meetings, parties, and private dining, pay only when you get booked.

**Target customer:** Venue owners/managers (bars, restaurants, event spaces, meeting rooms) rather than mobile event suppliers.

**Pricing model:** Free to list, no membership fees, no lock-in. Commission charged only on confirmed bookings: 10% for dining bookings in the US/Canada/Singapore, 12.5% for dining bookings in the UK/Ireland/Australia, and 15% for meetings/production bookings with mandatory online payment ([Tagvenue commission FAQ](https://support.tagvenue.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360018025377-What-is-the-Tagvenue-commission)).

**Marketplace capability:** Yes — core product; every listing is manually verified (photos, capacity, details) before going live.

**CRM capability:** None found — enquiry-based messaging only.

**Booking capability:** Enquiry-based, not instant-book: customer messages the venue, venue responds with availability/pricing; not a live calendar system like Poptop.

**Contracts and invoicing:** Commission is deducted automatically from the transaction; no evidence of broader contract/invoicing tooling for the venue's other business needs.

**Payment capability:** Yes, required for commission collection on meetings/production bookings.

**Automation:** None found beyond commission auto-deduction.

**Public profile or storefront:** Yes — venue listing pages with starting prices, per-person rates, minimum spend shown upfront.

**Reviews and verification:** Manual listing verification process; standard review functionality implied but not detailed in sources reviewed.

**Analytics:** Not documented publicly.

**Subscription justification without leads:** N/A — no subscription, pure commission-on-success model, identical structural logic to Poptop.

**Strengths:** Genuinely free to list; commission only on completed bookings (aligned incentives); manual verification adds trust; transparent published commission rates (rare in this space).

**Weaknesses:** No operational tooling beyond the transaction itself; enquiry-based (not instant) booking is slower than Poptop's model; niche to venues rather than the full range of event professionals.

**What ELBOLD must not copy:** Nothing egregious found — Tagvenue's transparent, published, success-only commission structure is arguably a best practice worth studying, not avoiding.

**Market gap left open:** Tagvenue shows that venues will accept commission-only monetisation happily when terms are transparent and success-aligned — reinforcing that the real gap is not "will suppliers accept a marketplace fee" but "is there anything to keep them engaged and paying between successful bookings."

## 7. Thumbtack

**Core promise:** Get matched with local customers actively searching for your service, across hundreds of categories (US-focused; not event-specific).

**Target customer:** Broad services businesses in the US (contractors, event planners, photographers, etc.).

**Pricing model:** Pay-per-lead, no subscription required for the core marketplace. Lead prices are dynamically set by Thumbtack's algorithm based on trade, metro, competition, and estimated job value — typically $8–$150+ per lead depending on category; pros can cap max price-per-lead and set a weekly budget ("Promote") ([Thumbtack Help Centre](https://help.thumbtack.com/article/pay-for-leads), [pricing analysis](https://pipelineon.com/blog/how-much-does-thumbtack-charge-per-lead/)).

**Marketplace capability:** Yes — core product; each project is typically shown to 4–5 (and reportedly up to 15) competing pros.

**CRM capability:** Light — in-app messaging, quote management; not a full CRM.

**Booking capability:** Limited — quoting/scheduling within a job thread, not a full calendar/availability system.

**Contracts and invoicing:** Not offered as a general business tool.

**Payment capability:** In-app payment option exists for some categories but is not universal/mandatory.

**Automation:** "Promote" auto-bidding/budget caps are the main automation feature.

**Public profile or storefront:** Yes — pro profile with reviews, credentials, portfolio.

**Reviews and verification:** Yes — reviews are central to the trust model; background-check badges available for some categories.

**Analytics:** Basic lead/spend reporting within the pro dashboard.

**Subscription justification without leads:** N/A — pay-per-lead only, same structural logic as Bark/Add to Event, but at significantly higher price points per lead.

**Strengths:** Massive US demand volume; sophisticated targeting/budget controls; strong brand recognition among US consumers.

**Weaknesses:** Widely and consistently criticised for low close rates relative to spend (one documented case: $3,753.83 spent on 20 leads, 6 refunded, only 1 job closed — a 5% close rate versus a claimed 30%+ industry benchmark); non-exclusive/shared leads billed to multiple pros simultaneously via a "share with more pros" mechanic; bad-lead refunds often issued as platform credit rather than cash, forcing continued spend; documented account deactivations following payment disputes ([Pipeline On](https://pipelineon.com/blog/how-much-does-thumbtack-charge-per-lead/), [handymanstartup.com](https://www.handymanstartup.com/thumbtack-pro-reviews/)). Trustpilot/SmartCustomer aggregate ratings are very low (around 1.8/5).

**What ELBOLD must not copy:** Charging multiple competing suppliers for what is effectively the same non-exclusive lead; issuing bad-lead refunds only as locked-in platform credit; opaque, algorithmically-variable per-lead pricing that suppliers cannot predict or budget against reliably.

**Market gap left open:** Reinforces the same gap as Bark/Add to Event — no accountability between spend and won business, and reviewers explicitly want lead exclusivity and real refunds, not credit traps.

## 8. Eventbrite

**Core promise:** The event-discovery and ticketing platform — create, promote, and sell tickets to an event, with access to Eventbrite's marketplace audience for discovery.

**Target customer:** Event *organisers* running ticketed events (workshops, classes, festivals, conferences) — a materially different customer from a one-to-one event *vendor/supplier*, but relevant as the dominant "event operations" incumbent.

**Pricing model:** Free for free events. For paid tickets: a service fee of 3.7% + $1.79 per ticket, plus 2.9% payment processing (in-person: $1 + 2.9%) ([Eventbrite Help Centre](https://www.eventbrite.com/help/en-us/articles/755615/how-much-does-it-cost-for-organizers-to-use-eventbrite/)). Subscription-style plans layer on top: Essentials ($0/mo, 2%+$0.79/ticket), Professional ($15–$100/mo, 3.5%+$1.59/ticket, expanded email marketing), and custom-priced Premium ([Eventbrite organizer pricing](https://www.eventbrite.com/organizer/pricing/)). Real-world fee load ranges from roughly 13.8% on a $25 ticket down to ~6.8% on a $1,000 ticket. Fees can be passed to attendees or absorbed by the organiser.

**Marketplace capability:** Yes — discovery placement in Eventbrite's ~90-million-user marketplace is a distinct, valuable feature separate from the ticketing infrastructure itself.

**CRM capability:** Minimal — attendee lists and basic email marketing tools at higher tiers; not a client-relationship CRM.

**Booking capability:** N/A in the vendor-booking sense — Eventbrite sells tickets to attendees, it does not manage one-to-one service bookings.

**Contracts and invoicing:** Not offered.

**Payment capability:** Yes — full payment processing is core to the product.

**Automation:** Email marketing automation at paid tiers; reminder emails, etc.

**Public profile or storefront:** Yes — organiser page and individual event pages.

**Reviews and verification:** Not a core feature (Eventbrite is not primarily a trust/reputation marketplace for suppliers).

**Analytics:** Sales/attendance dashboards are a standard, well-regarded feature.

**Subscription justification without leads:** Mixed — the $0 Essentials tier means most organisers pay nothing fixed; fees are transactional (tied directly to ticket sales), so there is little "zero-lead, still paying" exposure at the entry tier. Paid tiers exist mainly to unlock marketing reach, not core functionality.

**Strengths:** Massive, trusted brand for ticketed events; genuinely large organic discovery marketplace; fee-only entry tier removes fixed-cost risk; strong analytics.

**Weaknesses:** Per-ticket fee stack is high relative to ticket price for small/cheap events (~14% on a $25 ticket); not architected for one-to-one service bookings (weddings, private parties) at all — a structurally different product category from ELBOLD's core marketplace.

**What ELBOLD must not copy:** A fee structure that becomes proportionally punishing on lower-value transactions — small suppliers/small jobs get hit hardest, exactly the segment ELBOLD needs to retain.

**Market gap left open:** Eventbrite confirms that "fee only when you transact, zero fixed cost to start" is a viable and trusted model at scale for ticketed events — but it has no answer for one-to-one service vendors who need CRM/contracts/availability, which is outside its product entirely.

## 9. HoneyBook

**Core promise:** An all-in-one clientflow management platform — book clients, manage projects, and get paid, all in one place, for independent/small service businesses (heavily used by photographers, wedding planners, designers).

**Target customer:** US-centric (increasingly international) solo and small-team service businesses, strongly represented in the wedding/events/creative space.

**Pricing model:** Three published tiers, billed annually: **Starter** $29/month, **Essentials** $49/month, **Premium** $109/month (monthly billing is $36/$59/$129 respectively — 19–24% more) ([HoneyBook pricing](https://www.honeybook.com/pricing)). Payment processing: 2.9% + $0.25 (Visa/Mastercard), 3.4% + $0.09 (Amex/Discover/card-on-file), 1.5% flat (ACH).

**Marketplace capability:** None — HoneyBook does not generate leads for its users; it is purely an operations tool for leads the business already has.

**CRM capability:** Full — unlimited clients/projects, pipeline/status tracking, client portal, all plans.

**Booking capability:** Yes at Essentials+ — scheduler, calendar, lead capture forms (2 on Starter up to unlimited on Premium).

**Contracts and invoicing:** Yes, all tiers — proposals, contracts, invoices, payment plans.

**Payment capability:** Yes, all tiers — integrated card and ACH payments.

**Automation:** Yes at Essentials+ — workflow automations, SMS reminders, QuickBooks integration.

**Public profile or storefront:** Not a public discovery profile in the marketplace sense — client-facing portal only, branded to the vendor (branding removal itself is a paid feature at Essentials+).

**Reviews and verification:** Not applicable — not a discovery/trust marketplace.

**Analytics:** Basic reports (Starter), standard reports (Essentials), advanced reports (Premium).

**Subscription justification without leads:** **Strong — this is the core lesson for ELBOLD.** HoneyBook has zero marketplace/lead-gen function at all, yet retains a large paying user base because the entire client workflow (proposals → contracts → invoices → payments → automations) runs through the tool. Cancelling means rebuilding operational infrastructure, not just losing lead flow. Value is 100% independent of any leads the platform itself generates.

**Strengths:** Deep operational embedding; genuinely comprehensive feature set at a fair price point; AI features (lead alerts, email drafts, meeting notes) now included free across all tiers; generous 30-day trial, 60-day money-back guarantee.

**Weaknesses:** Documented complaints: fixed/inflexible branding on client-facing documents even on paid tiers; limited form customisation (no conditional logic, no CSS); reported feature regressions frustrating long-term users; short historical trial windows relative to Dubsado; ACH fee (1.5%) markedly higher than competitors; BBB shows billing-dispute complaints ([taskip.net pricing analysis](https://taskip.net/honeybook-pricing/), [Plutio comparison](https://www.plutio.com/compare/honeybook-vs-dubsado)).

**What ELBOLD must not copy:** Locking client-facing documents to platform branding at paid tiers (a friction point of exactly the kind ELBOLD's own vendors would resent); charging premium ACH fees relative to competitors.

**Market gap left open:** HoneyBook proves solo/small operators will pay $29–$109/month indefinitely for pure operational tooling with zero lead-gen — but it offers absolutely no path to new demand. A vendor who is good at delivery but weak at marketing gets no help here. This is precisely the gap ELBOLD's dual model (CRM *and* marketplace) is positioned to fill, provided the CRM half is as deep as HoneyBook's.

## 10. Dubsado

**Core promise:** A fully customisable business-management CRM for creative and service entrepreneurs — client management, forms, contracts, invoicing, and workflow automation.

**Target customer:** US-centric solo/small service businesses, especially wedding and creative professionals who want deep customisation over HoneyBook's more templated approach.

**Pricing model:** Two tiers, published: **Starter** $335/year; **Premier** $525/year (other sources cite monthly-billed equivalents of roughly $20/month Starter and $40/month Premier, which is not perfectly consistent with the annual figures — treat the annual figures from the official pricing page as authoritative: $335/yr ≈ $27.92/mo, $525/yr ≈ $43.75/mo) ([Dubsado pricing](https://www.dubsado.com/pricing)). Additional brands: $10/month each. Extra team users beyond the included 3: $25/month (4–10 users), scaling to $60/month (21–30 users). 21-day free trial with full Premier access, capped at 3 clients.

**Marketplace capability:** None.

**CRM capability:** Full — client portals, lead capture forms, project/communication history per client, lead-status pipeline tracking.

**Contracts and invoicing:** Yes, all tiers — invoicing, payment plans; public proposals at Premier only.

**Payment capability:** Yes — integrations with Stripe, Square, PayPal.

Because Dubsado is more customisable, switching cost (and thus retention) can be even higher once a business has built out its own automation logic.

**Market gap left open:** Same as HoneyBook — zero lead-gen. Confirms the pattern twice over: the market's best retention (operational embedding) and its best demand-generation (marketplaces) currently exist as two completely separate product categories with no crossover player of scale.

## Synthesis

### Three market archetypes, and nobody sits between them

The competitor set splits cleanly into three non-overlapping groups:

1. **Pure lead/booking marketplaces** (Bark, Add to Event, Thumbtack, Poptop, Tagvenue, and Hitched/Bridebook's discovery layer): monetise on leads or transactions, offer little-to-no operational tooling, and — critically — **structurally cannot charge when there's nothing happening**, because most (Bark, Add to Event, Thumbtack, Poptop, Tagvenue) have no subscription at all. The two that do charge a flat subscription regardless of lead flow (Hitched, Bridebook) are exactly the ones with the weakest supplier sentiment and the least ability to justify the charge in a quiet month.

2. **Pure CRM/operations platforms** (HoneyBook, Dubsado, Studio Ninja, 17hats, and Square Appointments for its paid tiers): monetise on a flat subscription with **zero** lead-generation function, and retain customers purely through operational embedding — the vendor's actual business (contracts, invoices, payments, client communication) runs through the tool, so cancelling is an operational cost, not just a marketing decision. This is the direct, validated answer to ELBOLD's core strategic question: **subscription value that survives a zero-lead month has to come from the vendor's business literally running on the platform, not from the promise of future leads.**

3. **Transactional/ticketing infrastructure** (Eventbrite): fee-per-transaction, no fixed cost at the entry tier, marketplace discovery bundled in — but built for ticketed events, not one-to-one service bookings, so structurally irrelevant to most of ELBOLD's vendor base.

**No competitor in this set combines group 1 and group 2 into one subscription that's independently justified by group 2's depth.** Bridebook comes closest (subscription + light CRM veneer) but its CRM layer is thin — auto-response templates and an enquiry inbox, not contracts/invoicing/payments/availability. This is the gap.

### The single biggest unaddressed gap

**Nobody has built a genuinely deep operating system (CRM + contracts + invoicing + payments + availability, at HoneyBook/Dubsado depth) fused with a genuinely functioning marketplace (at Poptop/Tagvenue transactional depth, not Bark/Thumbtack's low-trust pay-per-lead model) inside a single subscription.** Every marketplace player either charges nothing fixed (so has no "zero-lead month" problem to begin with) or charges a flat fee for pure visibility with no operational fallback (Hitched, Bridebook — and both draw the most negative supplier sentiment in this set for exactly that reason). Every CRM player has solved retention brilliantly but abandoned demand generation entirely. ELBOLD is structurally positioned to occupy the space between them — but only if its CRM half is actually as deep as HoneyBook/Dubsado (not a Bridebook-style veneer) and its marketplace half is actually as trustworthy/transactional as Poptop/Tagvenue (not a Bark/Thumbtack-style pay-and-pray model).

### Implications for ELBOLD's positioning

1. **The subscription must be sold — and priced — as if the marketplace didn't exist.** Every retained CRM competitor (HoneyBook, Dubsado, Studio Ninja, 17hats) proves vendors will pay $16–$109/month for operational tooling alone. ELBOLD's core plan needs to stand on that value proposition unaided; marketplace leads should be positioned as upside, not the reason to subscribe. If the CRM/booking/contracts/payments module isn't independently worth the monthly price against Dubsado/HoneyBook/Studio Ninja specifically, the "why pay in a zero-lead month" problem is unsolved regardless of marketplace quality.

2. **Contracts, invoicing, and payments are not features to gate behind a higher tier — they are the retention mechanism itself.** Dubsado's mistake (gating automation behind Premier) and 17hats' unresolved editor bugs both show that this exact workflow layer is where trust is won or lost. ELBOLD should treat this as core infrastructure, tested and reliable from day one, not a later add-on.

3. **The marketplace side must avoid the credit/pay-per-contact model entirely.** Bark and Thumbtack are the two most reputationally damaged platforms researched here (1.2–1.8/5 aggregate ratings), and the complaints are structurally identical: charged for the opportunity, not the outcome; shared/non-exclusive leads; refunds trapped as platform credit. Poptop and Tagvenue's success-only commission model (charge only on a confirmed, paid booking) is the credible alternative and should inform ELBOLD's marketplace commission design over any per-lead-credit mechanic.

4. **A published, transparent rate card is itself a differentiator.** Hitched's opaque, sales-negotiated pricing is a recurring complaint; Bridebook's and Tagvenue's public pricing pages are comparatively well received. ELBOLD should keep pricing fully public and simple — this reduces the "what am I even paying for" friction that erodes trust in a zero-lead month specifically.

5. **Vertical depth beats horizontal breadth for CRM trust.** Studio Ninja (photography-only) and Bridebook (wedding-only) both show that event professionals respond to workflows that match their specific job lifecycle, not a generic "service business" template. ELBOLD's CRM should ensure category-specific workflows (a caterer's booking lifecycle is not a photographer's) rather than one undifferentiated pipeline — this is a defensible moat neither HoneyBook nor Dubsado has built (they are deliberately horizontal), and it directly reinforces "why ELBOLD and not a generic CRM."
