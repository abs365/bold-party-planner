# Phase 5 — First Success Story Framework

**Date:** 2026-06-07
**Principle:** Only genuine marketplace activity. No fake reviews. No synthetic content.

---

## Purpose

Social proof is the single most effective trust signal available to a marketplace. Not feature lists, not promise copy, not badge counts. Real stories from real people who had real experiences.

The challenge at launch is that real stories do not exist yet. This framework describes how to collect them as soon as they do, and how to use them properly.

---

## What Counts as a Success Story

A success story is any genuine account of a positive outcome on the platform. There are four types.

**Customer testimonial:** A customer describes their experience finding, booking, and working with a vendor through ELBOLD. The key elements are: what they were trying to do, whether they found the right vendor, how the booking and payment process felt, and what the event was like.

**Vendor testimonial:** A vendor describes their experience of receiving enquiries, communicating with customers, and getting paid. The key elements are: whether the platform delivered what it promised, whether the process was straightforward, and whether they would recommend other vendors join.

**Booking success story:** A specific booking from quote request through to completed event, told as a short narrative. This does not need to be long. "Sarah booked a DJ for her daughter's 18th in Chelmsford. The vendor responded within 2 hours, the event went ahead, and Sarah left a 5-star review the next day." That is a success story.

**Event showcase:** A longer format piece, ideally with photographs, showing the event that was planned using ELBOLD vendors. With the customer's permission, this can be published on the website or social media.

---

## Collection System

### Immediately after every completed booking

The platform already sends a review request email 3 days after an event is marked as completed. This email should also include an optional invitation to share a testimonial.

Add a line to the `sendReviewRequest` email:

"If you would be happy to share a short sentence about your experience for our website, please reply to this email. We will never publish anything without your permission."

That is it. No form, no incentive, no pressure.

### For vendors, after first 3 bookings completed

Once a vendor has completed their third booking, send a personal email from the founder:

"You have now completed 3 bookings through ELBOLD. That is real. We would love to share your experience on our website if you are open to it. Would you be willing to write 2-3 sentences about how the platform has worked for you?"

This is a personal ask, not an automated prompt. It will receive a much higher response rate.

### Concierge requests that lead to bookings

When a concierge request results in a booking and then a completed event, follow up personally with the customer. These are the best quality testimonials because the customer came to you specifically because they needed help, and you delivered.

## What to Do With Stories When You Have Them

### Homepage

A single customer testimonial, displayed clearly below the Featured Vendors section or alongside The ELBOLD Promise section. One real sentence from one real customer is worth more than any amount of promise copy.

Format: Name, event type, city, quote. Photograph if available.

Do not display testimonials until you have at least 2 genuine ones. A single testimonial looks thin. Two or three looks like a pattern.

### Founding Vendor page

One vendor testimonial describing how the platform delivered bookings. This is especially valuable for vendor acquisition because it removes the "will this actually work?" objection from prospective applicants.

### Social media

Short clips or screenshots from customer reviews (with permission). Event showcase photographs. Behind-the-scenes vendor content that shows real professionals doing real work.

Do not post generic stock photography and call it an ELBOLD event. That breaks trust the moment anyone looks closely.

---

## What Not to Do

Do not invent quotes. Even paraphrasing or cleaning up a real quote without showing the original to the person is risky and unnecessary.

Do not publish any testimonial without explicit written permission. A reply to an email counts as permission if you ask clearly. A positive review on the platform does not automatically grant permission to republish it elsewhere.

Do not use star ratings as testimonials. "4.9 stars from 3 reviews" is not a testimonial. It is a number. A testimonial is a human sentence.

Do not create composite stories. "Many of our customers have said..." is not a success story. It is a vague claim that customers will not believe.

Do not offer incentives for testimonials. A review or testimonial given in exchange for a discount is not genuine social proof. More importantly, it is against the Competition and Markets Authority guidelines on fake reviews in the UK.

## First Milestones to Capture

These are the specific moments worth documenting as soon as they happen:

1. First vendor approved (screenshot of profile, congratulatory message to vendor)
2. First quote request received (internal note: time, event type, vendor response time)
3. First quote responded to (note: how long it took, what the vendor said)
4. First booking created (internal milestone)
5. First deposit paid (Stripe screenshot, booking record)
6. First event completed (mark in system, reach out to both customer and vendor)
7. First review submitted (publish on platform, consider reaching out for testimonial)

Each of these is a real business milestone. They are also the building blocks of your first success narrative, which you can use in press, in investor conversations, and in vendor recruitment.

---

## Timeframe

You cannot collect success stories before you have completed bookings. The sequence is:

Trust the process. Build the supply (vendors). Facilitate the demand (customers and concierge). Complete the first bookings. Collect the stories. Use them.

Do not skip to social proof before you have social proof. The temptation to show something before it exists is real and should be resisted.
