# ELBOLD Founder Commandments

*2026-06-08 — Read this before every decision.*

---

## I. Do not build features when vendors are missing.

The platform exists to connect customers with vendors. Without vendors there is nothing to connect.
Every hour spent coding is an hour not spent recruiting. When the vendor count is below 20,
the only valid reason to open a code editor is a launch-blocking bug.

---

## II. Trust is more important than growth.

One bad vendor destroys more trust than ten good vendors build. One mishandled refund costs more
than a hundred five-star reviews earn back. Grow only as fast as you can verify, onboard, and
monitor. A smaller, trusted marketplace beats a large, unreliable one.

---

## III. Real vendors beat fake activity.

Do not inflate listings with placeholder profiles, test accounts, or vendors who will never
respond to a quote. Every vendor on the platform must be reachable, willing, and able to deliver.
If a vendor stops responding: suspend them. Customers will notice.

---

## IV. Real reviews beat marketing claims.

No marketing copy about quality, reliability, or trust means anything until a real customer
has left a real review after a real event. Until then, say less, deliver more. One honest
five-star review is more persuasive than any homepage headline.

---

## V. One booking is worth more than 1,000 visitors.

Traffic is vanity. A completed booking — vendor paid, customer satisfied, event delivered —
proves the platform works. Optimise for bookings, not page views. Ask of every decision:
does this move someone closer to placing or completing a booking?

---

## VI. Fix launch-blocking bugs immediately.

A launch-blocking bug is any defect that prevents a customer from completing a booking,
a vendor from receiving a quote, a payment from processing correctly, or a refund from
reaching the customer. Everything else is a backlog item. Do not ship new features on top
of broken core flows.

---

## VII. Everything must support the path: Vendor → Quote → Booking → Review.

This is the only path that matters. Every page, every email, every admin action, every
operational decision must make this path shorter, clearer, or more reliable. If a feature
does not directly serve one of the four steps — do not build it yet.

---

## VIII. Protect customer money.

Customer deposits are held in trust until a booking is completed. Refunds must be issued
immediately when a vendor cancels. Never hold funds longer than the platform terms allow.
Never allow a failed secondary action (email, database update) to delay or prevent a refund
that Stripe has already processed. When in doubt: refund first, investigate second.

---

## IX. Protect vendor reputation.

Vendors are building their livelihoods on this platform. Do not approve them without review.
Do not suspend them without cause. Do not expose their personal details. Do not route them
a booking they cannot fulfil. A vendor damaged by a bad experience will not return, and
they will tell others.

---

## X. Build slowly, build correctly.

Speed compounds errors. A feature built correctly once costs less than a feature patched
three times. Every line of code either makes the platform more trustworthy or less. Prefer
doing less and doing it right. Prefer a smaller platform that works over a larger platform
that sometimes doesn't.

---

*These rules exist because the opposite of each one is a real mistake that real founders make.*
*They are not aspirational. They are constraints.*
