# ELBOLD Founder Identity Review

**Date:** 2026-06-08
**Purpose:** Recommend founder representation language that reflects family ownership, UK roots, and long-term trust — without creating personal dependency on a single individual.

---

## The Core Question

How ELBOLD presents its founders shapes:
- Whether customers trust the brand before meeting any vendor
- Whether vendors feel they are joining something permanent
- Whether the business can grow beyond any one person

A marketplace built on personal identity (a named founder's face and story) creates fragility. If that individual steps back, the brand loses its anchor. A family-owned business identity is both more accurate and more resilient.

---

## Current Representation Audit

The following pages currently reference the founder in some form. Each is assessed for tone and risk.

### /about

**Current pattern:** Likely references a founder story, personal motivation, the "why" behind ELBOLD.

**Risk:** If phrased as "I, [Name]..." — creates personal dependency. First-person founder voice reads as a solo operator, which can signal fragility or limit scalability to vendors evaluating the platform.

**Recommended shift:** Move from "I" to "we" and from named individual to family-owned business identity. The story of WHY remains powerful — it does not require a face or a name.

---

### /trust

**Current pattern:** Trust page likely references who stands behind the platform.

**Risk:** A single named person as the trust anchor means that trust is tied to one individual's ongoing involvement. If any personal circumstances change, customers may feel the business changed.

**Recommended shift:** "The Lawal family" or "The Lawal team" as the trust anchor. Family ownership signals continuity, shared accountability, and long-term commitment more effectively than a sole founder.

---

### /why-elbold

**Current pattern:** The founding motivation — typically written as a personal story.

**Risk:** Strong personal stories are powerful, but they should reflect a family's values, not an individual's journey. "My family has been planning events for years and we saw a gap" outperforms "I founded ELBOLD because..."

**Recommended shift:** The story stays. The teller becomes "the Lawal family" rather than one person.

## Recommended Representation Framework

### Primary Identifier: "The Lawal Team"

Use this in:
- About page author attribution
- Trust page ("Backed by The Lawal Team")
- Email signatures from the platform ("The Lawal Team at ELBOLD")
- Vendor outreach scripts ("You're joining a marketplace built by The Lawal Team")
- Social media bios

**Why "The Lawal Team" over a single name:**
- Signals collective ownership and shared values
- Implies support infrastructure — not one person's WhatsApp
- Allows the brand to survive any individual's absence
- Matches how family businesses present to clients in the UK ("The Johnson Group", "The Patel Family", "The Oke Partnership")

### Secondary Identifier: "Family-owned"

Use in:
- About page subheading or meta description
- Trust page context paragraph
- Vendor pitch ("ELBOLD is a family-owned UK marketplace")
- Footer ("Family-owned. UK-based. Built for the long term.")

**Why "family-owned" works:**
- Immediately distinguishes from VC-funded, disposable-platform energy
- Signals stability and accountability
- Has emotional resonance in the UK events market — vendors and customers both prefer to deal with humans, not anonymous corporations
- Authentic — it is the truth

### Tertiary: "UK-based, trust-led"

Use in:
- Homepage subheadings
- About page values section
- SEO meta descriptions
- Vendor acquisition outreach ("We're a UK-based, family-run team")

## Recommended Wording — Page by Page

### About Page

**Heading:**
"About ELBOLD"

**Subheading:**
"Family-owned. UK-based. Built to last."

**Body (opening paragraph):**
"ELBOLD was founded by the Lawal family — a UK-based team with a shared belief that the events industry deserved a marketplace built on trust, not volume. We set out to create something carefully curated: a place where event professionals are genuinely vetted, where customers feel protected, and where quality comes before quantity."

**Attribution:**
"The Lawal Team, ELBOLD"

**NOT:**
"Hi, I'm [Name] and I founded ELBOLD because..."

---

### Trust Page

**Key trust statement:**
"ELBOLD is built and run by the Lawal family — a UK-based team personally accountable for every vendor on this platform."

**NOT:**
"I personally review every vendor application."

**Why this works:** The family framing retains the personal accountability signal (humans are checking, not an algorithm) while distributing that trust across a team rather than a single person.

---

### Why ELBOLD Exists

**Opening:**
"We started ELBOLD because we believed the events industry needed something better."

**Core narrative:**
Use "we" and "the Lawal family" throughout. The founding story — the observation that the market lacked a curated, trust-first option — remains exactly the same. Only the voice shifts from first-person singular to first-person plural / family identity.

**Closing line:**
"This is our commitment to you — as a family, and as a business built for the long term."

---

### Footer

**Current (assumed):** "ELBOLD Ltd." or similar

**Recommended addition:**
```
Family-owned. UK-based.
Built for event professionals who care about their reputation.
```

Or a shorter version:
```
Family-owned UK marketplace for event professionals.
```

---

### Vendor Outreach Scripts

In all outreach messages (email, Instagram DM, WhatsApp, phone script), replace any reference to an individual founder with:

**Instead of:**
"Hi, I'm [Name], the founder of ELBOLD..."

**Use:**
"Hi, I'm reaching out on behalf of The Lawal Team at ELBOLD..."

or

"Hi, ELBOLD is a family-run marketplace we're building in London, Kent and Essex..."

This maintains the personal warmth of outreach while establishing that there is a team, not just one person.

## What NOT to Change

| Element | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| The founding story and motivation | Keep — it is authentic and compelling |
| The personal care and curation message | Keep — "we review every vendor" is a differentiator |
| The UK-specific, regional focus | Keep — London/Kent/Essex is the identity |
| Honest language about being early-stage | Keep — never claim to be bigger than you are |
| The warmth and directness of tone | Keep — this should feel human, not corporate |

---

## Things to Avoid

| Pattern | Why to Avoid |
|---|---|
| Named founder photo on every page | Creates personal dependency; if founder's circumstances change, brand feels different |
| "I personally guarantee..." | If the business grows, this becomes impossible to honour at scale |
| Sole first-person throughout | Signals solo operator, which may deter vendors seeking a stable platform |
| Claiming to be a "team" when there isn't one | Inauthenticity — "family" is honest and warmer than a fake team |
| Listing family members by name without consent | Maintain privacy — "The Lawal family" is enough |

## Summary

The transition from individual founder identity to family-owned business identity:
- Requires no operational changes
- Requires copy updates to 3 key pages (About, Trust, Why ELBOLD)
- Strengthens rather than weakens the personal, trustworthy brand positioning
- Protects the business from dependency on any single person
- Reflects the truth of how ELBOLD is actually structured

This is not depersonalisation — it is the correct scale of identity for a marketplace that intends to be a permanent fixture in the UK events industry.
