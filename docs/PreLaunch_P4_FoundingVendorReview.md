## Positioning Statement

Every piece of founding vendor messaging should be consistent with this:

"ELBOLD is a trusted event professional network connecting customers with verified DJs, photographers, caterers, decorators and more across London, Kent and Essex."

This is the honest, accurate description at launch. It does not claim national scale, hundreds of vendors, or a large existing customer base. It positions ELBOLD as specific, local, and trustworthy.

### Messaging Alignment Check

Before sending any outreach to new vendors, verify the following in each script:

1. Does it mention London, Kent or Essex? If not, add geographic specificity.
2. Does it claim a large existing customer base? If yes, remove or soften.
3. Does it reference a specific founding window (20 places, limited time)? Good — keep.
4. Does it mention free listing and 90% commission? Good — keep.
5. Is there an em dash anywhere? Remove all — use commas or full stops instead.

### Scripts That Likely Need Review

WhatsApp scripts: check for "hundreds of customers" or "thousands of bookings" claims.
Instagram DMs: check for scale claims.
Invitation email: check for "UK-wide" or "national marketplace" language.

If any of these exist, replace with specific, local language:
- "customers in Essex and London searching for your services"
- "families and couples in Kent planning events near you"
- "event hosts across London, Kent and Essex"

## Positioning Red Lines

Do not say:
- "UK's leading event marketplace"
- "hundreds of verified vendors"
- "thousands of bookings completed"
- "growing rapidly"
- Any numbers you do not have

Do say:
- "a curated network of verified event professionals"
- "serving London, Kent and Essex"
- "individually reviewed before going live"
- "one of the first 20 vendors on the platform"
- "building something from the ground up, together"

The honesty is a strength, not a weakness. Vendors who join because the platform is real and specific will stay and perform better than vendors recruited with inflated scale claims.
